# 07 — Brand and messaging

Owner: Marketing (agent 7), with UX (8), Community (13), Legal (10) and the Cynic (3) reviewing.
Date: 2026-11-24. Working name in this doc: **Handshake** until a new name clears legal.
Labels: **[D]** decided in `00_decisions.md`, **[A]** assumption, **[O]** open.

## 1. Positioning

**For** people who just met someone in person and want to keep talking,
**who** don't want to hand over a phone number or social handle to a near-stranger,
**[Name] is** a no-install chat you start by saying six digits to each other.
**Unlike** swapping Instagram handles or scanning a QR to a profile, nothing about
you is shared until you both choose it, one step at a time, and the chat
disappears after 14 days unless you both keep it.

One-line version: **"Say six numbers. Keep the conversation. Share the rest when you're ready."**

What we are not: not a dating app, not a social network, not a contact book, not
an ad business. We don't help strangers find you; we help two people who already
met keep talking.

## 2. Three personas

### Priya, 34 — the conference attendee
- Product manager at a 200-person company; attends 4–6 conferences a year.
- Meets 20 people a day; trades LinkedIn with half and regrets it with some.
- Wants: a quick "let's keep talking about that" with the speaker or the person
  in the coffee queue, without a lifelong connection request.
- Fears: inbox clutter, recruiters, being contactable forever.
- Moment of use: hallway after a session, 90 seconds before the next talk.
- Message that lands: *"A conversation that lasts as long as the conference does — or longer if you both want."*

### Marco, 41 — the regular at a neighbourhood venue
- Climbs three evenings a week at the same gym; knows faces, not names.
- Wants: to suggest a climbing session with someone without it feeling like a pick-up line.
- Fears: awkwardness, "giving my number to a guy I've belayed twice".
- Moment of use: at the bench by the table tent, after a session.
- Message that lands: *"No numbers, no handles. Just a chat at your gym — and you both decide what comes next."*

### Dana, 29 — the community organiser
- Runs a monthly board-game night (40–60 people) and a volunteer litter-pick group.
- Wants: newcomers to find each other and form small groups, without her running a WhatsApp group with everyone's number in it.
- Fears: liability, someone being made uncomfortable at her event, admin work.
- Moment of use: setting up the room; she puts the zone poster by the door.
- Message that lands: *"Help your people connect without collecting anyone's number — including yours."*

## 3. Voice

Familiar, happy, collaborative, calm. We sound like a friendly host, not a security vendor and not a flirt.

| We are | We are not | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Familiar | Jargon-y | "Say your code out loud" — not "Initiate a mutual verification" |
| Happy | Hype-y | "Nice — you're connected!" — not "Amazing!!!" |
| Collaborative | Bossy | "You both decide" — not "You must" |
| Calm | Alarmist | "Someone else entered your code, so we made you a new one" — not "SECURITY ALERT" |

Rules: short sentences; "you" and "we"; never "users"; no exclamation marks in
safety or privacy copy; no fear-selling; every claim literally true today.

## 4. Name options

"Handshake" is crowded: Handshake is a well-known US student-careers platform,
and the word is used widely in tech (protocols, payments, CRM tools). We would
spend money explaining we're not them. Below are three original options.
**Risk notes are from the team's own knowledge only — no trademark search has
been run yet (our web search was unavailable today). Legal must run a clearance
search (budgeted in December 2026, see `10_financial_model.md`) before we print anything.** [A]

### Option A — Sayso (recommended)
- Rationale: "On your say-so" means *with your permission*. It also describes the
  mechanic: you *say* your code *so* you can connect. Consent and speech in one
  warm, two-syllable word. Easy to say across a noisy bar.
- Tagline: **"Keep talking, on your say-so."**
- Risk: medium. It's a common English phrase, so it's probably been used for
  small apps or survey tools before (we believe at least one "SaySo"
  consumer-feedback product existed). Likely registrable for a narrow class
  (messaging software) if no live conflict; .com is likely taken, so plan on
  `getsayso` or `sayso.chat` [A].

### Option B — Bothways
- Rationale: nothing happens unless it goes both ways — both codes, both reveals,
  both say yes to new members. It explains the product's core rule.
- Tagline: **"Nothing's shared until it goes both ways."**
- Risk: medium-low conflict, but weaker distinctiveness (a plain descriptive
  phrase can be harder to register). Slightly less warm; can read as a dating
  term ("swings both ways"), which the Cynic flagged.

### Option C — Nearword
- Rationale: a coined word for "a word you say to someone near you". Most
  likely to be unique and ownable.
- Tagline: **"Say the word. Start the chat."**
- Risk: lowest conflict risk of the three. Downsides: needs explaining, less
  emotional, sounds a little like a vocabulary app.

**Recommendation: Sayso**, provided clearance comes back clean. Fallback order:
Nearword (if we want the safest legal path), then Bothways.

## 5. Key messages

1. **No numbers, no handles.** Say six digits, start a chat. Nothing else is shared.
2. **Both of you, every time.** A chat only starts when you both enter each other's codes. Each extra step (first name and photo, socials, phone) only opens when you both choose it.
3. **Light by default.** Chats disappear after 14 days unless every member taps "Extend". Either of you can end a 1:1 chat and it's gone for both.
4. **Small groups, with everyone's yes.** Add someone in person; the group approves first. Up to 8 people. New people only see messages from after they join.
5. **No app to install.** Open the page, say your code. Works on any phone browser.
6. **Built for venues and events.** A table tent gives your space a zone. Staff see how many people connected — never who.

## 6. The privacy promise (literal copy)

This text may be printed exactly as written. It only describes what the product
does today (see `00_decisions.md`). Do not shorten it into something stronger.

> **Our privacy promise**
> We never store location history, sell data or run ads.
> Your phone rounds your location to a square about 1 km across before sending it. We turn that into a zone number and forget it.
> Zone numbers live only on short-lived codes, which are deleted within about two minutes of expiring or being used.
> Our logs never contain codes, message text, zones or IP addresses.

Supporting lines we may also use (all true today):
- "No account, no phone number, no email needed."
- "Messages are encrypted when stored, and chats are deleted when they expire."
- "If you report a chat, you choose whether to include the last 20 messages. Reports are deleted after 90 days."
- "Venue staff see counts, never people. Fewer than 3 shows as 'fewer than 3'."

Lines we **must not** use: "end-to-end encrypted" (it isn't), "anonymous forever"
(you may choose to reveal), "we can't see anything" (the server stores
messages, encrypted, until the chat ends), "100% safe", "no data" or
"untraceable".

## 9. Viral loop

The product spreads person to person because every connection needs two phones.

1. **Exposure.** Someone sees a table tent, poster or event slide.
2. **First handshake.** They try it with a friend or a new acquaintance; the other
   person must open the page too. *Every handshake brings in at least one person
   who may be new.*
3. **Group growth.** A 1:1 chat becomes a group with "Add someone" — each add is another in-person handshake.
4. **Venue pull.** People who like it ask *other* venues and organisers, "Do you have a zone?" We give them a one-tap "Suggest [name] to a venue" page that produces a short note they can send themselves (we collect nothing).
5. **Venue push.** Staff dashboards show connection counts, which helps venues justify paying and keeping the tents out.

What we won't do to boost the loop: no contact-book upload, no "invite 5 friends"
rewards, no referral tracking of individuals, no forwardable invite links in the
MVP (decided off, see `00_decisions.md`).

Measured with aggregate counters only: handshakes per venue per day, share of
handshakes where one side is a first-time device [A — counted as a daily total,
not per person], chats becoming groups.

## 10. Launch campaign ideas

1. **"Six numbers" hallway game (conferences).** The organiser opens day one with
   "Find someone who…" prompts; pairs who connect get a sticker at the registration desk. Fits the Jan–Feb 2027 conferences.
2. **"The table word" (neighbourhood pilot).** Each week a new two-word table
   word (MAPLE, CHALK, TONIC…) is chalked on the venue board. Regulars learn to look for it. Low cost, high ritual.
3. **"No more napkin numbers."** Poster and social series: the napkin with a
   phone number, crossed out, next to "47-12-83". Light, funny, not fear-based.
4. **Organiser starter pack.** Free poster + 50 stickers + a 2-minute host
   script for community groups; "Bring your own zone" to meetups.
5. **Founder in the room.** Michael or the Community Lead hosts a "first Thursday"
   at each pilot venue for the first two weeks to show people how it works.
6. **Story wall (no identities).** Anonymous one-liners collected via a paper
   card at the venue ("We met at the 6B wall, now we climb Tuesdays") — see
   `11_pilot_plan.md` for how we collect these without identities.

Launch channels we will not use: paid social retargeting, influencer dating
content, any ad network.

## 11. Disagreements and how we settled them

- **Cynic vs Marketing on "safe".** Marketing wanted "the safe way to keep in
  touch". The Cynic: "Safe is a promise we can't keep. We can't stop someone
  being unpleasant in a chat, and we don't verify anyone in the MVP." Legal
  agreed: "safe" invites liability. **Resolved:** we never say "safe"; we describe
  the tools ("end a chat any time", "report", "nothing shared until you both
  choose").
- **Trust & Safety vs Marketing on dating imagery.** Marketing's first poster
  showed a couple at a bar. T&S: it frames us as a dating app and attracts the
  wrong first users. **Resolved:** imagery shows mixed groups, climbing partners,
  conference hallways; dating venues come later, after the pilot evidence.
- **UX vs Marketing on "anonymous".** Marketing liked "anonymous chat". UX: people
  reveal step by step, so "anonymous" becomes untrue mid-chat. **Resolved:** say
  "nothing shared until you both choose" instead.
- **Privacy Architect vs Marketing on "we forget your location".** Accepted only
  in the full literal wording in section 6, never as "we don't know where you are" (we briefly know your rounded square).
- **Cynic vs everyone on the name.** The Cynic would keep "Handshake" for the
  pilot and rename later to save money. Legal and Marketing disagree: renaming after
  printing materials costs more. **Resolved:** pick before printing in February 2027.
