# 09 — Sales kit

Owners: Sales (agent 6) and Business Development (agent 2), with Finance (12)
on pricing, Legal (10) and Trust & Safety (11) on objection answers.
Labels: **[D]** decided, **[A]** assumption, **[O]** open. **[name]** = product

**Golden rule for everyone selling:** only describe what works today. If a
feature is planned, say "planned" and give no date unless the Manager has
confirmed it. Never say "safe", "anonymous forever" or "end-to-end encrypted".

## 1. Conference organiser pitch

### 1.1 One-page script (about 3 minutes)

**Open (15 s).** "Hi [Name], thanks for the time. Quick question first: after
your last event, how did attendees keep in touch with people they met?"
*(Listen. Usual answers: LinkedIn, business cards, the event app nobody opened.)*

**Problem (30 s).** "That's what we hear too. Swapping LinkedIn feels like a big
commitment for a two-minute hallway chat, and event apps need a download and a
profile. So most good conversations end at the coffee queue."

**What we do (45 s).** "[name] lets two people keep talking by saying six numbers
to each other. Open a web page — no app, no account — and your screen shows a
code like 47-12-83. You say yours, they say theirs, you both type them in, and
you're in a private chat. Nothing else is shared. If you get on well, you can
both choose to share first names and a photo, then socials, then phone numbers,
one step at a time. Chats disappear after 14 days unless everyone keeps them.
And you can grow a chat into a small group of up to 8 — everyone approves new people."

**Why organisers like it (30 s).** "You get a themed event zone that only works
during your event hours, signs and badge stickers, a slide for your opening,
and a dashboard showing how many connections and group additions happened each day. You'll
never see who connected with whom — nor will we sell or share anything. That
makes it easy to say yes to with your privacy team."

**Proof (20 s).** "[Use only real numbers: 'At our pilot, X handshakes over 8
weeks'. Before we have numbers, say: 'You'd be one of our first three founding
events, so the price is $1,500 regardless of size, in return for a short case
study using counts only.']"

**Ask (20 s).** "Could we put it in front of your attendees at [event]? If it
helps, I can send a one-page summary and a sample dashboard today."

**Close.** Agree a next step with a date: a call with their attendee-experience
lead, or a signed order form.

## 2. Venue pitch

### 2.1 Counter script (about 2 minutes, ideally at a quiet hour)

"Hi, I'm [Name] from [name] — is the manager around for two minutes?

You've got regulars who see each other every week but never swap numbers,
because that feels like a lot. [name] gives your space a zone. We put a table
tent here with a QR code and a weekly word. Two people who want to keep talking
just say six numbers to each other and they're in a private chat — no app, no
numbers, no handles.

For you: people linger, come back, and start groups that meet here — climbing
partners, quiz teams, Thursday regulars. You get a dashboard showing how many
connections happen each day. Never names, never who. We don't run ads and

Staff need five minutes of training — basically 'point at the tent'.

Can I show you on my phone? [Demo with a colleague or second phone: 30 seconds.]

## 5. Objection handling

| # | Objection | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | "Is this a dating app?" | "No. It's for anyone who met in person and wants to keep talking — climbing partners, conference contacts, quiz teams. There are no profiles, no swiping and no way to find strangers. You can only chat with someone you've spoken to face to face." |
| 2 | "What if someone gets harassed?" | "Anyone can end a 1:1 chat in one tap and it disappears for both people. Anyone can report; they choose whether to attach the last 20 messages. Our team can suspend a device so it can't make new codes. Nobody's phone number or name is shared unless both chose it. We won't promise it can never happen — we promise fast, simple tools and a same-day response to venues." |
| 3 | "Do you track my customers?" | "No. We never store location history, sell data or run ads. Your dashboard shows counts per day — never names, never who talked to whom — and small numbers show as 'fewer than 3'." |
| 4 | "Why not just use QR codes to Instagram?" | "That hands a stranger your whole profile, photos and friends list forever. With us, you share nothing until you both choose, one step at a time — and the chat fades after 14 days unless you both keep it. Plenty of people won't give out their Instagram after one chat; they will say six numbers." |
| 5 | "Nobody will download another app." | "They don't have to. It's a web page. Scan the QR or type the address, and your code appears." |
| 6 | "Couldn't someone use our posted code from home?" | "The table word changes every week, the zone only works in your opening hours, and the code you say aloud lasts 60 seconds. And a chat only opens if both people enter each other's code, so there's nobody to reach from home." |
| 7 | "What if someone overhears my code?" | "If a second person enters your code, it's cancelled, you get a new one and a short alert. Nobody is connected unless you also enter their code." |
| 8 | "Is it legal / what about GDPR or privacy law?" | "We collect very little: no names or numbers unless you share them inside a chat, no location history. We have a privacy policy and terms reviewed by counsel [A — before launch]. Happy to send them to your privacy team." |
| 9 | "What about under-18s?" | "It's 18+. Everyone confirms their age on first visit. We don't sell to events aimed at under-18s." |
| 10 | "$79 a month is a lot for a small café." | "It's about one regular's coffee a week. Try it free for 30 days; if your dashboard shows nobody's using it, cancel. Annual prepay gets 2 months free." |
| 11 | "My staff don't have time for this." | "Five minutes of training. Their only job is to point at the tent. We change the weekly card for pilot venues." |
| 12 | "What if it causes problems in my venue — liability?" | "People are talking in your venue already; we don't change that. We never claim the product makes anyone 'safe'. We do give you a direct line to us and a same-day response, and we can pause your zone instantly." |
| 13 | "Our event already has an app." | "Keep it. Ours does one thing their app can't: lets two people keep talking without profiles or downloads. Most event apps get opened by a minority of attendees; ours runs in the browser from a badge sticker." |
| 14 | "How do I know it's working?" | "Your dashboard shows connections and groups per day. Events get a summary within 48 hours." |
| 15 | "Will people's chats disappear after the event?" | "After 14 days unless everyone in the chat taps 'Extend'. That's on purpose: light by default." |
| 16 | "Can I see who's using it, to reward regulars?" | "No, and that's by design. We can't show you that because we don't collect it." |
| 17 | "Is it verified? How do I know people are real?" | "Today, no ID checks — it's based on meeting in person. An optional verified badge (yes/no only, via a third-party ID check) is planned, not yet live." |

## 6. Discovery questions

Organisers:
1. How many attendees, and how many first-timers?
2. What do attendees say they want most — meeting people, content, jobs?
3. How do attendees keep in touch today? What breaks?
4. Who owns attendee experience, and who signs off on privacy?
5. What would make this a success for you — a number, a quote, rebooking?
6. What's your budget line for engagement or networking?
7. When do you lock suppliers for the next event?

Venues:
1. How many regulars do you see in a typical week? Which nights are busiest?
2. Do regulars form groups — teams, partners, clubs?
3. Do you run themed nights? Which hours?
4. What would make a monthly fee worth it to you?
5. Who decides spending — you, an owner, head office?
6. Any past incidents or worries about customers bothering each other?

Community / campus:
1. How many members or students, and how do newcomers meet people now?
2. Do you run welcome weeks or recurring events?
3. Who handles safeguarding and data protection?
4. Are any of your members under 18?

## 7. Sales process stages

| Stage | Exit criteria | Typical time | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Target | Fits profile (events 300+, venue with regulars, community 50+) | — | BizDev |
| 2. First conversation | Discovery questions asked; pain confirmed | 1 week | Sales |
| 3. Demo | Live 30-second handshake shown; decision-maker identified | 1–2 weeks | Sales |
| 4. Proposal | Price, dates, zone plan, privacy one-pager sent | 1 week | Sales + Finance |
| 5. Trial / pilot | Venue: 30-day trial live. Event: zone configured, materials shipped | 2–4 weeks | Community Lead |
| 6. Closed | Signed order form or first invoice paid | — | Sales |
| 7. Renew / expand | Event rebooked or venue on month 3+; ask for referral to another organiser | ongoing | Community Lead |

Pipeline rules: log only business contacts (organiser, manager) in the CRM —
never end users. Follow up within 3 working days. Lost deals get a one-line reason.

## 8. Disagreements

- **Sales vs Legal on objection #2.** Sales wanted "we keep everyone safe". Legal
  and the Cynic refused. **Resolved:** answer describes tools and response time only.
- **Sales vs Finance on founding price.** Sales wanted founding events free.
  Finance: free events don't test willingness to pay. **Resolved:** $1,500 flat,
  first three only.
- **Sales vs Privacy Architect on dashboards.** Sales asked for "returning
  customer" counts. Privacy declined — it would need per-person tracking.
  **Resolved:** counts per day only.
